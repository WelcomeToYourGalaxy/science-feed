What this is. A feed on research integrity worldwide, in twenty subjects, scoped to what the section it follows counts. Fabricated and falsified data. Paper mills and papers bought by the manuscript — the section describes one operation running over a hundred manuscripts a month with a hundred staff, three hundred freelancers across six countries and more than a thousand agencies feeding it. Plagiarism. Authorship bought, gifted and ghosted. Image and figure manipulation. P-hacking, salami-slicing and outcome switching. Citation cartels and metric gaming. Undisclosed AI generation. Undisclosed conflicts. Then the two things that make all of it compound: detection lagging by years while fraud is produced faster than it is caught, and every fraudulent paper contaminating the work built on top of it. Then peer review and what it catches, predatory venues, replication, who profits from the record, who can read it, who funds the research, the incentives that reward volume, the people who catch it, and what is being built to fix it. Nothing here is written or summarised: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own, every row links to the original, and no model touches the text.

This is a feed on research integrity, not science news. A discovery is not the subject. "Scientists discover", "new study suggests", "breakthrough in" — all refused, along with the space, fossil and eclipse coverage that fills most science pages. What this wire keeps is what is wrong with the record and what is being done about it, so no term stands alone: "retracted" counts beside a paper or a journal, "peer review" beside failure or fakery, "authorship" beside sold, gifted or disputed.

Two things worth saying about scale. The section's own figures are estimates of very different confidence — a survey of self-reported misconduct, a publisher's screening rate, a screening study of one year's papers, and a modelled figure for the literature as a whole. This wire does not average them or pick one. It carries the reporting and the primary sources, marks what carries a measured figure, and leaves the arithmetic to you. And where the section quotes an AI estimate for the share of compromised literature, that is a guess by a language model and is treated as one — the wire tracks what is measured, not what is estimated by something that cannot count it.

Standing. Every wire is labelled by what kind of source it is, and every row carries the label. Funders, institutions & integrity offices is the bodies that investigate and rule — research integrity offices, universities, funders and courts. Journals, publishers & metascience is the record itself and the research on it, including retraction tracking. Open science & accountability groups is those pushing for change, and the sleuths who find most of it first. Press is general news across 26 languages. Standing is provenance, not a claim that a story is important.

Weight. Separately, each story is scored on what it carries: a decision — a retraction, a misconduct finding, a dismissal, a degree revoked, a journal delisted, a prosecution (2); institutional material — an integrity office finding, a funder or publisher investigation, a peer-reviewed study or preprint, official statistics (2); a measured figure such as a retraction count, a flagged-submission rate or a share of papers (1); a pending decision with a date attached (1); a named jurisdiction (1); and a primary source (1). At three or more the row is marked consequential.

What is refused. Discovery coverage and the science pages — breakthroughs, new species, telescope images, eclipses, health tips — along with business filler, sponsored content and market copy. The status line reports how many stories each harvest refused.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Region. Placement runs three levels deep. Pick a region and a second row opens with its subregions; pick one of those and a third row lists the places inside it — Latin America to the Amazon Basin to the Brazilian Amazon, Oceania to Australia to the Northern Territory. Each row counts what it holds, so an empty subregion is visible rather than hidden, and every story files under the subregion and region above whatever place it names.

Limits worth knowing. The gate is mechanical: it reads words, not meaning. Standing is assigned per wire rather than per article. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days. Coverage is uneven by language and the counts show it rather than hiding it.

Every wire read on the last harvest, with its standing: what it returned, and how many of its stories were refused. A wire that could not be reached says so rather than disappearing.
